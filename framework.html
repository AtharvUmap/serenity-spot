Course Frameworks

A Framework for Change

The Serenity Spot is more than a planting project. It's an exercise in three frameworks that have shaped my thinking this semester: the 8Cs of the Social Change Model, the Social Change Wheel, and Deepa Iyer's Social Change Ecosystem.

Social Change Model

The 8 Cs

Seven values move from the inside out, the individual, the group, the community, and converge on the eighth: Change itself.

Individual Values

C1

Consciousness of Self

Knowing your own beliefs, feelings, and motivations.

In my project

I came into this work already knowing my values: honesty and trust, authenticity, and commitment. Growing up across Singapore, Malaysia, and India, and learning in international schools, I saw early how diverse experiences and perspectives generated new ideas, and how much an environment could either nurture or constrain that. UMD's North Campus, with its scarcity of green and social space, felt like one of those constraining environments. Recognizing that mismatch was what brought me into this project.

C2

Congruence

Acting in line with your beliefs, consistently.

In my project

For me, authenticity and Congruence were the same idea. Knowing what I valued was only step one; being intentional and in-line with those values, going a step beyond self-awareness, was what gave them meaning. As Co-President of Club Cricket, I had practiced this by ensuring my words and actions stayed aligned, and I carried that same standard into the redevelopment proposal.

C3

Commitment

The energy to keep going through friction.

In my project

Commitment was one of the values I led with. From Club Cricket and from group projects, I had seen the drastic difference in synergy between teams whose members were genuinely passionate about the mission and those who weren't: the first group generated initiative and ideas, the second waited for someone else to act. This project asked for the first kind of energy, and I was committed to bringing it.

Group Values

C4

Collaboration

Working with, not for or alongside.

In my project

Trust was the precondition for real collaboration, something I had practiced as Co-President of Club Cricket by fostering strong relationships with new and returning players alike. Even though this was an individual project, the work asked me to collaborate with experienced people who were the best at what they did, including the Vice President for Mental Health and Wellbeing and a senior campus planner. I learned to listen first, defer to their expertise where it mattered, and bring a student perspective where it was missing.

C5

Common Purpose

A shared "why" the group keeps returning to.

In my project

Even though this was an individual project, the actors I spoke with all returned to the same orientation. The Vice President for Mental Health and Wellbeing, the senior campus planner, and the other people I consulted shared a purpose around improving and developing the spaces students live in. That convergence gave the work a clear direction: a more nature-driven campus that imbued calmness, peace, and unity.

C6

Controversy with Civility

Disagreement held respectfully, in service of better outcomes.

In my project

Navigating different perspectives was a constant in a project this broad. Each campus actor I spoke with held their own vision for what the pathway could become, and it was my responsibility to take in all of their suggestions and shape them into the final vision. Civility, here, meant making sure every viewpoint was heard and weighed before I made the call.

Community Value & the 8th C

C7

Citizenship

Belonging to, and being responsible for, a larger community.

In my project

Citizenship ran through this project. I had long been an advocate for the international community at UMD and a believer that diverse perspectives generated new ideas, but the campus environment had to make that exchange possible. Proposing an adaptation of the pathway between the Computer Science Instructional Center (CSI) and the Brendan Iribe Center (IRB) was, for me, an act of belonging to North Campus and taking responsibility for it. This project allowed me to take responsibility for the UMD community as a whole.

C8

Change

The hub where the other seven Cs converge, change in self, in group, in community.

In my project

The change was concrete: a corner of UMD's North Campus, the pathway between CSI and the Iribe Center, reshaped to be more nature-driven, more cozy, and more conducive to meeting new people than what was there. The upstream change was harder to point at, treating campus environment design as a mental-health intervention, not just a beautification project.

Toolkit

The Social Change Wheel

Social action takes many forms. The Wheel pushes us to ask: which forms of action is this project actually doing, and which ones could it reach for next?

Spoke 1

Direct Service

Hands-on work that meets a need now.

My work

Direct, on-the-ground design work: walking the site, drawing visual maps of where the changes could land, and selecting seating and tables that aligned with UMD's campus standard.

Spoke 2

Advocacy

Speaking up for a policy or institutional change.

My work

The proposal as a whole was an act of advocacy: speaking up for better seating, better space, and a campus environment that supported student wellbeing.

Spoke 3

Community Engagement

Co-creating solutions with the people most affected.

My work

The redesign was scoped to reach beyond a single user group. The pathway between CSI and the Iribe Center is travelled by students from across campus, with a particularly heavy concentration of computer science students, so the work was anchored in serving that broader community.

Spoke 4

Education & Awareness

Helping others see the issue.

My work

This website and the proposal as a whole were built to do more than describe one project. The hope was to show other students that a campus change like this is reachable, and to inspire them to take on initiatives of their own.

Spoke 5

Civic Engagement

Working through institutions and processes.

My work

Working through campus institutions and processes: engaging the Facilities Student Advisory Subcommittee, the Architectural and Landscape Review Board (ALRB), and navigating UMD's broader approval process.

Spoke 6

Philanthropy

Resourcing the work, money, materials, time.

My work

Resourcing the work meant going where the funding lived. A submission to the Student Facilities Fund was the route to financing the redesign and putting student dollars behind a student-driven space.

Spoke 7

Community Organizing

Building durable power among neighbors.

My work

The underlying aim of the project was to create more social space for interactivity and community. Where seats and shade exist, organic organizing follows: study groups form, conversations begin, and a campus grows its own community over time.

Spoke 8

Socially Responsible Behavior

Personal practice that models the change.

My work

The personal energy I put into this project was its own form of practice: investing in a space I would not personally use forever, in the hope that it would resonate with and serve a wide range of students for years to come.

Deepa Iyer's Framework

Roles in the Social Change Ecosystem

No project moves with one kind of person. Building a physical space made these roles visible, every one of them was load-bearing.

Visionary

"What if?"

Imagines a future not yet here, and refuses to let the group flinch from it.

Who held this

Atharv Umap (me). The enthusiastic idea generator who kept finding new directions to take the project further.

Weaver

"Who else?"

Connects the dots between people, departments, and prior efforts.

Who held this

Atharv Umap (me). Compiling knowledge from people across departments, from Landscape and Special Services to Well-Being to Facilities Planning to Leadership Studies, and weaving their input into a single proposal.

Experimenter

"Let's try."

Tests in small, low-stakes ways, proves it can work.

Who held this

Atharv Umap (me). Running the small prototypes, sending the form out across the Computer Science department, and reading what the 34 responses said about how the change would actually land.

Builder

"Brick by brick."

Makes the systems, structures, and physical things that hold the work.

Who held this

Dr. David Myers, Associate Professor of Plant Science and Landscape Architecture, and the studio group that could pick this up next semester to design the space, brick by brick.

Caregiver

"How are you?"

Tends to the people doing the work, and to the work itself.

Who held this

Dr. Warren Kelley, Senior Associate Vice President for Well-Being. His role on campus is care, and he tended to this project the same way: pointing me to the Student Facilities Fund and reminding me to anchor the proposal in real student demand.

Storyteller

"Here's why."

Makes the work legible, to peers, to administrators, to future cohorts.

Who held this

Atharv Umap (me), through this website. Making the proposal, the timeline, and the reasoning behind it legible to anyone who wants to pick the work up next.

Disruptor

"Why not?"

Names what's broken; refuses default answers.

Who held this

The ALRB and the FSAS. The Architectural and Landscape Review Board and the Facilities Student Advisory Subcommittee named the gaps I had missed and the conditions I had to meet, and refused to let the proposal move forward until those things were in place.

Healer

"Repair."

Tends to the wounds, historical, relational, ecological, that the work touches.

Who held this

Pamela Scott, my instructor. The fallback point and mentor who helped me work through every challenge the project threw up, and the steady voice when the path forward was unclear.

Guide

"Here's how."

Has done it before. Offers wisdom and warning.

Who held this

Ms. Karen Petroff, Director of Landscape and Special Services. She walked me through the practical considerations any campus greenspace has to answer, and pointed me to the right people for each next step the project needed.

"An important shift was realizing that I didn't need to be every role. The project asked different things of me at different moments, and other people were already standing in the gaps."
